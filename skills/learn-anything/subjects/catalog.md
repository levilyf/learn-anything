# Subject catalogue

This is the discovery index for the Learn Anything subject library. It is a map of learning areas, not a claim that every listed module is already written.

**Status meanings:** Planned = catalogued, not authored; In progress = being authored or reviewed; Available = module exists; Validated = passed the quality gates in [the ecosystem blueprint](../docs/subject-ecosystem.md).

## Domain map

### 1. Technology and computing
- AI literacy, generative AI, prompt design, AI evaluation, AI agents, automation, machine learning.
- Programming: computational thinking, Python, JavaScript/TypeScript, data structures, algorithms, testing, software design.
- Computer science: operating systems, architecture, databases, networking, distributed systems.
- Web development: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, frontend, backend, APIs, accessibility, deployment.
- Data: spreadsheets, SQL, statistics, visualization, analytics, data engineering, machine learning.
- Infrastructure: Linux, cloud, DevOps, containers, reliability, observability.
- Security: digital safety, networking, secure development, application security, defensive operations, governance.

### 2. Business and finance
- Entrepreneurship, customer discovery, business models, validation, pricing, operations, strategy.
- Marketing, sales, branding, product management, customer support, negotiation.
- Personal finance, budgeting, saving, credit, taxes, investing literacy, risk.
- Accounting, bookkeeping, financial statements, cash flow, business finance.
- Economics, markets, decision-making, business analytics.

### 3. Academic foundations
- Mathematics: arithmetic, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, calculus, discrete mathematics, probability, statistics.
- Natural sciences: physics, chemistry, biology, earth and environmental science.
- Formal reasoning: logic, scientific method, research literacy.
- Humanities and social sciences: history, geography, civics, psychology, sociology, philosophy, economics.

### 4. Languages and communication
- English foundations, grammar, vocabulary, reading, writing, listening, speaking, pronunciation.
- Additional languages with level-aware paths and communicative practice.
- Public speaking, debate, storytelling, technical writing, professional communication.

### 5. Design and creative work
- Visual design, typography, color, layout, illustration, branding.
- UI/UX, interaction design, research, prototyping, usability and accessibility.
- Photography, video editing, animation, 3D, creative writing, music theory.

### 6. Human and professional capabilities
- Critical thinking, analytical reasoning, problem-solving, decision-making.
- Learning how to learn, memory, research, information literacy.
- Productivity, collaboration, leadership, conflict resolution, negotiation, career planning.

### 7. Practical and maker skills
- Electronics, hardware, repair, robotics, automation, fabrication.
- Cooking, household skills, personal organization, practical safety.
- Project planning, experimentation, measurement, troubleshooting.

## Stable subject IDs

Use lowercase kebab-case IDs. Once a subject has learner records or external references, do not casually rename its ID. A renamed display title does not require an ID change. Split or merge subjects only with a documented migration path.

## Scope and status rules

- A catalogue entry is not a finished course.
- A module is not **Validated** until it passes the quality gates in the blueprint and has been reviewed against current sources.
- Subjects may link to shared prerequisite modules instead of duplicating their content.
- Keep the core tutor generic. Subject-specific safety rules, tools, and pedagogy belong in the subject module.
- Accept learner requests as demand evidence, but do not let one loud request outweigh broad usefulness and content quality.
